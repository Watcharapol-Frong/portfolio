## The starting point

Constraint is the whole idea here: no photography, no illustration, no gradient. Just type, weight, and negative space doing all the work.

## Why black and white first

Removing color forces every decision to live in scale, spacing, and contrast. A poster that still reads at a glance in grayscale has earned the right to add color later.

![A bold sans-serif poster layout with heavy negative space](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1509343256512-d77a5cb3791b?w=1200&q=80)

The eye has nowhere to hide in a layout this sparse - every kerning mistake shows.

### Choosing the one typeface

Pick a single family with enough weights to create hierarchy on its own, rather than mixing typefaces to do that job.

## Wrapping up

Minimalism isn't the absence of a decision - it's making one decision so confidently that nothing else is needed.
