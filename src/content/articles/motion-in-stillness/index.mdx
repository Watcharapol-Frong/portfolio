## The starting point

A photograph has no duration, yet some images feel like they're mid-motion. That illusion comes from a handful of repeatable visual cues.

## Reading direction as a cue

Elements angled or streaked along a consistent axis read as "in motion" even when nothing in the frame is actually moving.

![Long-exposure style image suggesting speed and motion blur](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551288049-bebda4e38f71?w=1200&q=80)

Blur intensity and direction tell the eye how fast something was supposedly moving, and from where to where.

### Freezing versus implying

A sharp, frozen peak-action shot and a soft motion-blurred pan communicate different kinds of speed - pick based on the feeling, not just the subject.

## Wrapping up

Stillness and motion aren't opposites in an image - motion is often just stillness with the right visual cues pointing somewhere.
